Building StravAnalysis

June 25 2026


As a runner without the Strava premium subscription, performance-based and recovery-based metrics have always been important to me. As my final project for AP Computer Science approached, I began to think of what would be a unique, seamless, and realistic project. We only had 5 weeks to complete the project, so I had to make sure that my program was able to function with less features, but also leave room for growth. After talking with my groupmates, we decided on using the Strava API and analyzing activity and performance data.


This decision came after I noticed a big problem with current fitness analysis platforms. Apps like Strava, Garmin, and various others display analysis for workouts, but they do not offer personalized feedback and easy-to-understand visuals. This is largely from their attemps to "do too much", as some statistics can be difficult to analyze for beginners. Even though this is such a noticeable problem in the running and fitness world, Not many people have found solutions to this problem.


StravAnalysis aims to do what current apps lack, visual respresentation and quantifying qualitative data. The app creates graphs of important running metrics, like fatigue and chronic vs acute workload, create race predictions that have strong accuracy, and calculate training effect and recovery. As we began, we decided on a three page style: Dashboard, Activities, and Analysis.


The dashboard page provides an overall summary of current training progress, including weekly metrics and streak metrics, and graphs of mileage and chronic workload.


The activities page includes individual pages for each activity, as well as heart rate zones and zone analysis, which helps athletes understand what each workout benefits.


We considered training our own models for the Analysis page, but chose to integrate the Gemini API instead to be able to build a complete product.

At the end of the project, we had a working program that could analyze strava activities, which was the goal. Even though the goal was achieved, there are definitely many things that could be improved. Authentication was the biggest hurdle along the way, and it can sometimes get complicated when different operating systems use the app. Also, Strava is planning to make their API subscriber-only, which will inevitably stop the project. Even with this, StravAnalysis will continue to be improved and it has definitely made an impact on the way I see my running data.
